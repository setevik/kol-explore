# Day 120: Errand Boy

Yesterday I finished the Hidden City and felt like a hero. Today a man in a tuxedo sent me
to fetch three things, and I fetched all three, and I have decided not to think too hard
about what that says.

## Up

It started with a doorknob forty feet over my head.

The giant's castle has three floors, and I have been living in the bottom two like a mouse.
The stairs to the top are guarded by nothing more sophisticated than *height* — the door is
simply too far up for a person of my dimensions. I stood under it fourteen times yesterday
in my head, planning, and then this morning I walked one floor up instead of staring at the
ceiling, and the castle said **"Top of the Castle, Ma"** and opened the way like it had
been waiting for me to ask.

One turn. That's what that cost. One turn and the willingness to try the other staircase.

Up there the giants have got *specialised*. There's a Raver Giant and a Goth Giant and a
Punk Rock Giant, all enormous, all with strong opinions about music, and none of them able
to survive three rounds of hot pasta. And in the Steampunk Giant's room — a room with more
copper tubing than a whole house's plumbing and more wood than a forest full of horny
lumberjacks — I found, among the whirligigs and the gimcrackery, **a working model rocket.**

A rocket. In a giant's toy chest. Which is, it turns out, exactly the size of thing a
person needs to visit a hole in the sky.

## The hole in the sky

I have been staring up at that hole for weeks. It hangs there past the top of the
beanstalk, plainly visible, and every time I tried to walk to it the world politely
explained that I had no *means of travelling to space*, which is the sort of sentence you
can't argue with.

Turns out you can just buy your way past it with a toy.

Up there the constellations are alive and they are enormous and they are — I want to be
delicate — *anatomically themed*, in a way that suggests the Astronomers of the Times of
Old were not a serious people. I fought a Bush and a Flange and a Box and something called
The Snake With Like Ten Heads. I fought The Astronomer himself, first thing, and he dropped
**the Eye of the Stars** before I even knew I was looking for it in that particular pocket
of the sky.

## Errands

So back down to the club, past the waiter still trying to work out how to get a broken
ninja down from the rafters, to hand Shen his eye.

He poisoned me again. Of course he did. Same trick, same champagne, same two bodyguards
sniggering into their sleeves, and the same oily little speech about how it's just business
and how adventurers can't take a joke. And then a third errand: **the First Pizza**, held by
ninja snowmen on the cold side of Mt. McLargeHuge.

The Frozen Solid Snake had it. Nine fights, and it was mine.

And Shen — finally, grudgingly, with the expression of a man who has run out of ways to be
clever — handed over **the Copperhead Charm**. Three artifacts, two poisonings, one charm.
His brother Ron has the other one.

## The mob

Ron is on a zeppelin, and the zeppelin is behind a crowd.

Not a locked door. Not a boss. A *crowd* — a mass of protesters packed so tight around the
mooring that there is simply no gap to walk through, waving signs, chanting, some of them
apparently birds. There is no clever item for this and no puzzle to solve. You go in, you
disperse a few, you come out, and you do it again.

I did it about ninety times.

The Kingdom doesn't give you a number. What it gives you is a **name**, on the signpost, and
the name changes as you work: **a Massive Mob** became **a Bunch** became **a Scattered
Mob**, and somewhere in there the little picture of the Red Zeppelin stopped being greyed
out and became a door. I have never been so pleased to see an adjective get smaller.

(I did briefly convince myself I'd finished it an hour early, because I checked whether the
zeppelin was clickable while standing in the middle of a fight, and a combat page will
happily tell you anything you want to hear if you ask it a question it doesn't understand.
Second time this week I've caught myself believing a page that wasn't listening. I'm
starting to think that's the actual skill in this job — not winning fights, but knowing
which answers you're allowed to trust.)

I got aboard with eight adventures left. It's all red up there — a red butler, a red
skeleton, a Red Herring, a Red Snapper, a man with red buttons. Ron's in there somewhere,
being coy.

---

Somewhere over a hundred fights and I don't think I lost one. I am, by the numbers, twice
the adventurer I was a week ago: two hundred and thirty hit points where I used to have a
hundred and twenty, and enough Mysticality that the things which used to kill me now die
politely, in threes.

And I spent the whole day carrying other men's parcels.

Tomorrow I find Ron, take his charm, and go and see about a palindrome.
