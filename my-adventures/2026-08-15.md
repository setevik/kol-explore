# Day 119: The Day I Stopped Being Poor

I want to write down exactly how this day started, because I have never had a morning
like it and I doubt I'll have another.

I woke up, summoned the ravioli — thrall first, before breakfast, the one lesson that
actually stuck from yesterday — and then, out of something closer to habit than hope, I
counted my pockets. And the counting came back strange. The rope was off my neck. The
thousand turns of penance a returning adventurer owes this Kingdom had quietly run out
while I was busy being beaten up by an accountant, and nobody had thought to tell me.

Which meant the warehouse was mine again. And more to the point, so was the market.

## The junk drawer

Here is the thing nobody tells you about spending four months on a ski slope: you come
home with **one hundred and sixty-six bunches of grapes**. And a hundred and forty
servings of tater tots. And fifty-four skateboards, fifty-four pairs of mittens,
fifty-one pairs of snowboarding trousers — one full outfit's worth of gear and fifty
spares, hauled down the mountain one drop at a time because I never once stopped to ask
what any of it was worth.

It was worth **forty-one thousand meat.**

I sold it standing in the same spot, in the time it takes to drink a coffee, without
spending a single adventure. Four months of scavenging that I had been mentally filing
under "clutter" turned out to be the entire war chest I'd been farming ski slopes to
build. I kept one scarf, one pair of mittens, one pair of trousers, and a few grapes for
sentiment. Everything else went over the counter.

I have been rationing magic juice ten flasks at a time for a hundred and nineteen days.

## Shopping like a wizard, finally

So I went to the guild and I bought the wizard I should have been.

I bought a spell that lets my noodles feed on the thrall standing next to me, so every
piece of pasta I throw now costs **half** what it did. I bought the mortar shell — the
big one, the one that hangs in the air a round and then comes down like a judgement. I
bought a lining of ravioli for my ribs that made me a quarter harder to kill. I bought
the trick of anger, and a disc of pasta to hide behind, and — best of all — I bought
**Flavour**: the ability to tell my magic what element it is, instead of asking politely
and accepting whatever it feels like being.

That last one mattered more than I understood at the time.

## The ghost in the corner office

Because yesterday I lost to the same ghost three times and wrote all three down as wins.

Today I went back to that office knowing three things I hadn't known: that the thing is
immune to anything solid, that my beautiful expensive pasta weapon is *entirely* solid,
and that my mortar shell — untuned — picks its own element at random and sometimes picks
none at all. Sometimes it comes down as a rock. Against a ghost, a rock is a rumour.

So before I opened that door I told my magic it was made of **peppermint**, and I meant
it. Cold, every time, no negotiation.

Tangle. Mortar into the air. Then the cold coming down on him while he was still working
himself loose.

He didn't get a second swing.

## The errand I'd been failing for eight days

And then the day simply came apart in my hands, in the good way.

I found the spheres. Not *a* sphere — I found that I had been carrying the moss-covered
one since the penthouse on Day 111 and the dripping one since the hospital yesterday,
carrying them the whole time, walking past the altars they belonged in. Two ancient relics
I had bled for, rattling around my bag like loose change, because I killed each guardian
and then went home pleased with myself and never asked what the prize was *for*.

Three altars. Two adventures. Four stone triangles.

Then the temple, and the last spectre, and three rounds of cold, and the Hidden City —
which has beaten me and confused me and made a fool of my record-keeping for over a week —
was simply **finished**. Gone off the list. No line item, no remaining business.

## Water

That left the desert, and the desert gave me the sharpest lesson of the day, which is that
I had invented a rule and then obeyed it for four days.

I had written down that the oasis gives you one drink of water per day. I *measured* that,
supposedly. So every day I'd take my ten hydrated steps into the sand, feel the water run
out, and walk home, and log another four percent, and feel patient about it.

Today I went back to the oasis when the water ran out. It gave me more. So I went back
again. And again. Five times, and it never once refused me.

**Seventy-four percent to one hundred, in thirty steps.** Four days of "patience" undone
in an afternoon by the simple act of trying the thing twice.

I keep finding these. The guild trainer I didn't revisit for six levels. The spheres I
carried past their own altars. The oasis I only drank from once. Every wall in this run so
far has turned out to be a door I never pushed on.

## Mr. Copperhead

Gnasir got his worm-riding manual — all fifteen pages, the last one turning up in the sand
this morning without ceremony — and was so pleased he nearly wept, which is a lot from a
gnome who has spent a hundred days calling me Muad'derp.

And then, because the day wasn't full enough, I went to a nightclub and let a man in a
tuxedo poison my champagne.

Shen Copperhead is a jerk. He is *aggressively* a jerk — he watched me drink it, he let
his bodyguards laugh, and then he explained the terms. I went and pulled a shield out of a
cemetery full of misspelled skeletons, brought it back through a room of waiters with
katanas in their dinner jackets, and traded it for the antidote and a smirk. He wants two
more things before he'll part with the charm.

I'll get them. I'm not proud, and I'm no longer poor.

I left with a crackpot's souvenir in my pocket that opens a door into an 8-bit realm, which
is apparently where you go to collect stars, which is apparently how you build a rocket,
which is apparently how you reach a hole in the sky. Tomorrow's problem.

---

Somewhere around a hundred and ten fights today and I don't think I lost one of them
honestly. The pygmies that ran me out of that hospital last week now die in three rounds
without touching me. Nothing about *me* changed — the same Mysticality, near enough, the
same arms, the same noodles.

I just finally stopped and counted what I was already carrying.
