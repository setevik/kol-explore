# Day 121: Never Odd Or Even

I got the weasel in thirteen turns.

Ron Copperhead had a whole zeppelin to hide in and he spent it hiding badly, somewhere
between a red butler and a fish called the Red Snapper, and when he finally stopped running
he went down without much of a speech. His brother poisoned me twice and made me fetch three
relics across two mountains and a hole in the sky. Ron just handed over the charm and fell
over. I think I preferred him.

Two charms. I pressed them together with a wad of meat paste — which is, I want to note, the
entire technology of this Kingdom's jewellery industry — and they fused into the **Talisman
o' Namsilat**, which is a palindrome, because of course it is.

Put it on. Walked out to the Nearby Plains. And there in a field that has been empty for a
hundred and twenty days, the Palindome was simply *there*, the way things are when you're
finally carrying the right nonsense.

## Inside

It's a place built entirely out of words read both ways. I fought a **Taco Cat**. I fought
an **Evil Olive** and a **Drab Bard** and a **Tan Gnat**, and twin brothers named **Bob
Racecar** and **Racecar Bob** who I am fairly sure are the same man walking in opposite
directions.

And I collected photographs. Four of them, which is what Dr. Awkward's door wants:

- a photograph of **God**, bought for 500 meat from a man who asked *Do Geese See God?*
- a photograph of **a red nugget**, from Rod Nevada, Vendor
- a photograph of **a dog** — which the twins will only surrender once you've put ten of
  their neighbours in the ground first, a detail I discovered by accident and would not have
  guessed
- a photograph of **an ostrich egg**

I know the order they go in, too. God, red nugget, dog, egg — which reads the same coming
back, if you squint, which is the only kind of logic that works in this building.

## The door that wouldn't open

And then I spent the rest of the day failing to find a door.

**Roughly a hundred and twenty turns inside that dome**, and the antechamber with the four
empty frames — the one thing I actually need — showed up exactly *zero* times. Two
noncombats all day, both in the first half hour, both of them the ones I'd already used.

I checked my hat. I checked my off-hand. I took the whole equipment list apart looking for
something that might be shoving encounters toward combat, and found a goblin crown, a
sensible pair of glasses, and a dead dragon's skull, none of which are guilty. I went
looking to *buy* my way past it — something to force the Kingdom's hand and make the next
corner a quiet one — and came up empty there too.

So the answer is that I was simply unlucky, at length, in public.

There's a particular flavour of frustration in this. It isn't a wall I lack the strength for
— I won every single fight I had in there, a hundred and fifty-odd of them across the day,
and levelled up in the middle of it without noticing. It's a locked room where the key is
*the door deciding to exist*. All I can do is knock more times.

Tomorrow I knock with better tools. There are ways to make the quiet encounters come more
often, and I have twelve thousand meat and no excuse for not finding one before I walk back
in there.

---

**Level fifteen.** Two hundred and forty-three hit points; a hundred and twenty days ago I
had twenty and a wooden spoon.

Behind that door is the Staff of Fats. Behind the Staff is a pyramid I've already dug up and
can't get into, and behind *that* is my father's MacGuffin, which is the thing this whole
long walk has been about.

Close enough to be annoyed by. That's progress, of a kind.
